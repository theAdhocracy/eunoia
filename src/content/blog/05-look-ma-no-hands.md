So yesterday we felt the full force of a 757 during take off; today we decided to experience the other end of the spectrum and take a ride in Sean’s little Cherokee. We had hoped to do some fly-bys over Anguila and the reefs beyond it but unfortunately constant squalls and generally poor weather conditions had us bogged down to the ground. If England didn’t appear to be under ten metres of snow at the moment I would have sworn we were back in the ‘Lisle!

At any rate we received last minute confirmation from the tower to do a shorter journey just around St. Maarten itself, which was a pretty spectacular way to tour the island.
For those without prior knowledge, the plane Sean flies is a pretty small aircraft with only a single engine but it seats four with relative ease. Jo was working today so only the three of us went up (and as Sean pointed out the weight levels were such that a fourth person couldn’t have come at any rate) with myself “co-piloting” and Mike relegated to the backseat to take photographs (more on that later).

As I was sat in the front I had to wear a headset which meant being able to hear the airport towers, which was pretty different. More importantly though, it meant I could have a go flying! I’d like to say I performed barrel rolls and loop-the-loops but in reality I was too terrified to tweak the yoke any more than a fraction at a time. Nevertheless it was pretty exhilarating; hopefully there will be a next time so Mike can give it a go! It was pretty funny how close the feeling of controlling the Cherokee and controlling fighter planes in Battlefield 1942 (a video game for the uninformed) actually was despite the complete difference in control and the added sensation of turbulence and engine roar.
