So once again we find ourselves without any real update for quite a while. I think it may be time to concede that this blog could become a more weekly than daily occurrence. Unless, of course, our lives get a whole lot more interesting…

Briefly mentioning our last post, the first Eunoia Vlog, we had a lot of feedback which was great to see, even if it was mostly constituted of “we don’t want to leave feedback” statements. Also, one recent request was to take some photos of J&S’s house and just more photos in general. In honesty we’ve never stopped taking them, just the time to sort them out and upload them has been lacking. Still, head on over to the gallery and we have some of the Carnival photos up with a few more albums of piks coming tomorrow or Wednesday; we’re also planning something a bit different regarding the house tour.

Yes indeed, Sunday was Carnival on the French side of the island so we went over to take a peek. I have to say, it really gave us a flavour of local culture; advertised to start at 2pm we trucked up at 4pm and still had to wait thirty minutes for anything to begin. Welcome to the islands!

I think the photos pretty much sum up the atmosphere; it’s no Rio but the costumes were still pretty extravagant and certainly colourful and man alive do they enjoy their bass! Every group of dancers or float would be broken by a giant music truck with amps and speakers stacked 6metre+ high on the back all ramped up to maximum volume so when they went by you really <em>felt</em> the euro-trash beat. I think there was some form of competition going on but we didn’t stick around to find out who won.

The only other piece of pseudo-news would be from today. It’s a Monday, yet Mike and I were not at work like good little boys. No once again we were down at the beach, jetting out to Creole Rock with scuba gear just so one of us could sit on the boat and watch the other one having all the fun. Only this time it was Mike’s turn!

As you probably already gathered from the Thought to the right of this article, I have finally managed to equalize and dive. It should be noted that Mike had no issues, he just opted out as it would have cost extra so amused himself hovering above me with a snorkel and occasionally free diving down and grabbing my head when I drifted within his reach.

Beyond the elation at finally managing to get down there, the actual diving was pretty poor. We’ve had rough seas now for quite a while so visibility was pretty restricted and I spent most of my time either taking part in manoeuvres on sea grass (which isn’t very comfortable) or trying to make sure I maintained equalisation. I did see some fish, but mainly just young reef fish with the occasional parrot fish thrown in; unfortunately I missed the octopus! Nevertheless, at one point, swimming by a large boulder, I looked up to see a shoal of Sergeant Majors (common black and white striped, rounded reef fish) hovering just above me. I think I sort of understood why people can really get into scuba diving at that point.
