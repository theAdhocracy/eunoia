The world is in the middle of a recession right now ‒ it’s kind of hard to miss. Yet despite this two eighteen year olds (well one 19 year old as of the time of writing; Happy Birthday Mike) have flown half way round the world to a tropical island in the Caribbean with golden beaches, palm trees and gentle, crystal blue seas.
The purpose of the above isn’t purely to invoke jealousy, it’s more to put into greater context the fact that today we decided we were a little bored of this tropical island so indulged a little further and popped over to Statia (Sint Eustacius) for a few hours. Yep it’s all blood, sweat and tears at this end, honest!

Of course we couldn’t fully justify Mike, Sean and I just flying over for the fun of it, so we did a little sight seeing (Sean even showed us J&S’s possible future residence: the local OAP centre) and a brief history lesson at one of the best preserved and oldest forts in the Caribbean. It was a pretty interesting little island really: owned by Holland, it was the first country in the world to recognise America as an independent state; going back even further, Statia was the first Caribbean island to have a free port making it the wealthiest place in the archipelago for a long time; despite being owned by the British during the War of Independence many of the American supplies and guns were bought on Statia; and now it’s a major oil depot with about half the island being consumed with huge oil tanks and piping to route it out to the large tankers parked off its coast.

We should have some photos up soon. Unfortunately I took quite a lot and sorting through them is proving far more time consuming than expected. The trip was pretty fun, even if we only did stay there for a brief time. For me it was the first place so far on the world tour that I had never been to before, so that alone made the trip worthwhile. On top of that, flying back to St. Maarten around 2pm on a Saturday is quite different as a lot of the big international flights were coming in. That meant we had a Jetblue Boeing land in front of us and a big American Airlines 757 coming in from behind as we landed ourselves which was quite… different.

All in all it was just interesting to see what a “real” Caribbean island looks and feels like, especially as we could just go without any prior planning. I guess that’s the benefit of owning a private plane!
