If today had one downside, one slight piece of negativity, it would have to be the organisation. Of course, as is so often the case, it is no one parties fault but rather a collection of little titbits of negligence that resulted in something greater than the sum of its parts. Whether it was the lack of communication from Aqua-Mania who have otherwise been so great, the short-sightedness on our behalf or the failure to convey basic information from the check-in desk it was a disjointed morning at best.

What we should have done, using that beautiful commodity known as hindsight, is stayed a night on the island of Saba. You see today was our big Caribbean treat; the expensive one off that weeks of $4 chicken satay lunches and walking instead of catching the bus had been building towards. Today we went to Saba, one of the nearest islands and one of the top dive sites in the world. We went there to dive; yet to achieve this we had to catch a ferry, had to book with a dive shop, hire gear, get up in the morning and have enough cash to cover ourselves.

Had we stayed the night we could have climbed the mountain through layers of rainforest, spent time on one of the more unique Antillian islands and dived at the world famous Pinnacles. However, we did not spend the night. Hindsight is a beautiful thing. Actually in hindsight we should have asked about the possibilities of lunch...

Yet, to turn this article on its head, despite the hunger, the tiredness, the sunburn and the feeling of just missing a trick, today was fantastic. It was fantastic simply because every millisecond of annoyance was completely and totally eclipsed by a single minute, comprised of 60,000 milliseconds, of complete and utter tranquillity. A tranquillity that only one creature can truly create. I've snorkelled with them, watched them in zoos, even touched one...but never have I swam side-by-side with one, in its element, in its own time. I talk, of course, of a green sea turtle.

The first dive of the day can be covered by Mike, suffice to say that Tarpin are some of the strangest and most impressive fish I have ever encountered. But it was the second dive, after the non-existent lunch and the worry of spending so much to have equalisation issues, the second dive that, for me, realised everything I had hoped scuba diving would be. As we twisted and turned down under water rift valleys, coral embankments towering up either side, spotting a lobster here, an eel there, a turtle idly drifting by above, it all suddenly became infinitely worthwhile.

Thus as we ploughed back through relatively calm seas on the locally-dubbed "Vomit Comet", watching the occasional flash of silver wings as flying fish sought safety from this unknown predator, I find it easy to think: hindsight be damned! I've swam side-by-side with turtles in the Saban sea; try saying that ten times fast.
