First things first, allow me to both apologise and explain regarding the prior blog. It was written at 11pm at night; we'd been travelling for nearly six hours and I hadn't really slept the night before (an experience I can assure you I won't be repeating). Furthermore, upon arrival in New York we were once again caught up in their confusing arrival forms and then were ushered rapidly onto a subway train without any real knowledge of how the NYSubway system worked or where, for that matter, this train was headed. All we knew was that we had to be at 95th street to go to bed and that we'd like to do that as soon as possible, thank you very much. This being the case when we noticed the street numbers going down instead of up, we presumed we'd hopped onto a train going in the wrong direction; our carriage had no display map of the subway system either so, panicked, we leapt off at the first stop. We studied a map for some time and came to the conclusion that actually, we had been on the correct train and the next one wouldn't be by for quite a while. Furthermore we were in Brooklyn and not Manhattan, hence the incorrect street numbers...obvious really. Oops.

In conclusion therefore we arrived late, having spent nearly two hours catching trains here and there, emerged onto the streets completely lost and stumbled our way somehow to the hostel and crashed. What little energy I had left went into finding a free WiFi spot to piggyback on long enough to post what, at the time, felt like an hilarious means of conveying our arrival. In hindsight it was pretty awful.

But enough about that; it was our first night and getting lost was probably always going to happen. We've worked out the grid format of Manhattan (and the number/letter algorithm the subway goes by) so now can navigate pretty easily, something we've used well the last two days to, well, go just about everywhere in the borough. Some may have noticed the tweets from day one coming in; if you had you would know we went to the highest point in NY, the Empire State building. It's pretty impressive but I would imagine a whole lot more exciting at night...hence the planned trip today up the TopoftheRock building.

We've been to Times Square (neon lights gain so much more meaning when they're several stories high), the NY Public Library, American National History Museum (actually too big for it's own good; it takes a lot to make me bored of animals and fossils - yet still exceptionally well presented and maintained), we've seen the Statue of Liberty from the Staton Island Ferry (easily the best bargain in NY as it cost us a grand $0) and walked around City Hall, the Washington Monument, Ground Zero, Central Park, the Chrysler building, Grand Central station, Chinatown, Soho, Little Italy and a couple hundred blocks of weird, antique flats and churches surrounded by towering sky scrapers in chrome and tinted glass.

We've eaten bagels from food stalls, choked on the high salt content of pretzels the size of your head, been ripped off by a hot dog van and discovered that true NY subs taste nothing like Subway...they're a whole lot nicer.

Unfortunately despite all our good experiences there has been one constant: walking. We've taken the subway a few times but so far no taxi's or buses...just lots and lots of walking. My feet are not happy. Not at all. But oh well, they just have to put up with one more day of wear and tear because after that we have a full two and a half days of sitting around on planes to go through. Oh joy...
