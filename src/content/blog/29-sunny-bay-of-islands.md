Today has been interesting. To begin with we absolutely <em>had</em> to be awake, packed and ready for 7:15am on the dot to catch our first KiwiExpress bus; knowing full well I often struggle with mornings I set my alarm for 5.30am. I promptly woke up at 3.30am...and that was that ‒ alarm rendered utterly superfluous.

We then had our first experience of trying to make sure we had everything packed whilst doing so without waking any of the other 8 people in the room and without any form of light. Finally the bus was late and when we did board, it was only to realise that the sky was overcast and looking stormy the way we were headed. Needless to say that the "sunny" Bay of Islands has yet to live up to its propaganda.

Luckily we have two nights booked here at Paihia so hopefully tomorrow will be all clear and we can head out on a traditional Maori war canoe for a mornings jaunt around some of the closer isles. The area has 140 islands in total, almost that many golden beaches and thousands of dolphins (plus a few orca and whales). On friday we hope to see a few of those on a four hour cruise out into the ocean proper, but for now the horizon would be enough.

Enough of Paihia though; as you should by now understand we have left Auckland behind, and not a day too soon. Auckland is a pretty amazing city with a real mixture of cultures and influences but it's not exactly thriving with activities. The main tourist venue would be the Sky Tower, which we decided wasn't worth out money when there are some pretty awesome bungy sites on our trip to come, so apart from the awe-inspiring park (Central Park eat your heart out!) there really isn't much to do.

That said the rest of New Zealand looks to be quite the opposite; we now have a rough plan of where we're going, when and for how long. We even roughly know costs of most activities we want to do and where to do them and if there's one thing I'm certain it's that we'll leave Kiwi wanting more time and already planning a return trip. If the Bay of Islands during awful weather can still look pretty incredible and even your basic farm land looks lush and full of strange birds and wildlife, I cannot wait to see the untouched areas!
