Back in the Big Smoke, or Auckland to everyone else, after a surprisingly eventful and fun few days in Paihia; getting some exercise certainly didn't come a moment too soon!

We kicked it all off with a Waka tour, or canoe trip to most people, just as the sun was beginning to shine on the Bay of Islands. The Waka were the original war canoes that the Maori people made and used, first to actually cross the oceans to colonise New Zealand and later as a means of transport both for trade and war. True Waka would be 50ft or longer and made from hollowed out tree trunks; ours was about the right length but constructed from fibreglass making it a whole lot easier to paddle and manoeuvre. Still we had two genuine Maori tribesmen with us for the trip and could listen to them chatting away about the "sweet as round Tiger had played" at the weekend (Tiger Woods obviously) or the hotel one of them was taking over in the Scilly Isles next week. The trip was surprisingly fun as we paddled our way clear across the bay to the historic town of Russel - first capital of New Zealand and one-time "Hellhole of the South". We'd thought of catching the ferry across later that day anyway so to suddenly have a real guided tour from locals who knew all the little details was certainly worthwhile and saved us a little bit of cash.
The return trip was a little harder as we were rowing against the tide and the wind but we seemed to fly along none-the-less. We even managed to catch a small fish on the way; catch it and then promptly drop it back overboard...oops.

After the Waka tour we spent an hour or so just wandering before hiring some bikes and heading out to find the Huararu Falls. An hour later we discovered them after winding through barely used mountain gravel tracks...on a main road. I wouldn't really class them as too spectacular, they were about the same size as the falls in the River Irthing back home. Still it was pretty different biking with the occasional mangrove and tree fern to look at.

Finally, after a nights sleep in the very nice YHA in Paihia we had just enough time to indulge in a cruise around the Bay of Islands looking for dolphins and sites of cultural importance. We did eventually find both, although the four bottlenose dolphins we found appeared to capture peoples attention far more than the myriad stories of bloodshed and war that the Bay appears to have as a cultural backbone.

And now, as stated, we find ourselves back in the middle of suburbia with cars and pub going revellers. It was a bit of a shame to leave Paihia but at least tomorrow we get to head off to Mercury Bay and a day of sea and surf. Well, you know, always find the silver lining \*grin\*.
