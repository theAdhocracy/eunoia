They call New Zealand the adrenaline capital of the world and we're fast discovering why. In the last article we wrote about kayaking, biking and cruising; we used to think of these as active pursuits (well maybe not so much with cruising) but now they can only be described as tame.

Yesterday we travelled from the banality of Auckland through to the homeliness of Mercury Bay and in doing so got our first taste of life aboard the Kiwi Express. I have to say I'm impressed so far, both with the service and the helpfulness of the drivers. Every driver we've had so far has been informative about the local area, culture and history as well as a very useful source of knowledge for both planning and booking parts of the trip. Our current driver, Damo, is pretty chatty but he seems to have a good eye for the decent activities which has been helpful.

The other aspect of Kiwi I've been impressed with is that it is far more than a drive from A to B; it's an entire tour. So rather than just route down the motorway yesterday we spent some time detouring to Mt.Eden for great views of Auckland and both oceans either side of NZ; we stopped at a great little bakery in Thames for lunch and even spent three hours on a beautiful beach, Cathedral Cove. This was the first adrenaline rush and I'm sure in time won't even compare.

To set the scene the waves were high, the beach shelved and rocks were scattered around. Only six of us swam, all lads, we dove through the waves and spent about an hour being hurled around, catching the occasional good wave but normally surfing it a little and then being chucked under (hopefully not onto a rock). It was great fun and a pretty decent start to the trip really.

Then from Mercury Bay, or Whitianga as it is locally known, fresh from a good nights sleep and bacon butty we took off for Rotorua. On the way once again we had a list of stops, such as Matamata; now more commonly known as Hobbiton due to the LotR films, as well as a brief walk into an old gold mining area that delved through cliffside tunnels used by prospectors 100 years ago.

Fortunately though we arrived in Rotorua with plenty of daylight left which allowed us to discover our first New Zealand special: luging. A Luge is a small cart made from fibre glass; you have minimal control, just some pressure breaks operated with the steering wheel and obviously steering ability. The rest is left to gravity.

To begin, head up a mountain on a gondola, grab your helmet and cart and pick your route back down. There were three options on this course: Scenic, Intermediate, Advanced. We all began with Scenic and worked our way up (our ticket was for 5 runs) but by the time we hit Advanced a group of us had formed a racing squad. We would grab our cars and head off, only to stop once out of sight of the officials, line up in grid positions and kick off. Bombing down the Advanced we had a few mishaps but nothing too serious, just the occasional graze. However for our last run we chose the Scenic as it was the longest; we took off like the Advanced run but instantly began pushing for speed on the shallower slopes. Bad choice, two corners later and a pile up occurred. Still no one was badly hurt and we set off with only a few more bumps and collisions on the final run - Mike won two, I placed once in second (the only time I beat him 😛) but it was hilarious and a great ice breaker for getting to know some more people.

So now we've tried Luging, what next? Who knows, but with everything from skydiving (yeah right) to black water rafting (not quite as extreme as it sounds) on offer really only our budget and nerves are limiting factors.
