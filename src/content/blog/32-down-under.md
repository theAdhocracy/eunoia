Thus the adventure continues as we delved underground into the world of black water rafting. First off it should be noted that blackwater rafting isn't much like white water rafting; for one thing there are no rapids, for another its in the dark and finally there's no raft - just inner tubes that you float around in.

However the rafting wasn't the real reason that we had entered the Abyss, as it is known in Waitomo,but for the whole adventure experience. To begin we dropped through a hole in the side of a hill, abseiling down 40m into the pitch black underworld. From there a brief trek through glowworm lit caverns to a drop of unknown depth where we were re-harnessed and sent off on a flying fox through the gloamy darkness without head torches or any natural light.

A brief stop followed for flapjack and coffee before we were fitted out with inner tubes (personally rather unsuccessfully; far too small) and leapt off a cliff edge into the 3'C subterranean river below. Sitting in the tubes, paddling frantically upstream both for warmth and to stop them flipping over there was little to do but watch out for overhangs and gaze at the glowworms all around us.

After a while we came to a point where the roof was too low to continue so our guide linked as up in a chain and began dragging us back, torches off to better see the strange blue pinpricks of light the worms created. Eventually we came to the point we had leapt in and abandoned our tubes to begin wading further downstream, minding out for the occasional eel.

Our route to the surface had begun as we slid down one small rapid and then climbed up a side shaft, through a few tide squeezes, to emerge at a two tier waterfall we were told originated from a pool up above. Guided slightly, we pulled ourselves up the sides of the falls using the water-worn handholds for grip and eventually emerged into starlight in the Kiwi bush.

It had certainly been three hours of adventure underground; unexpectedly for me it had also been three hours of great fun.
