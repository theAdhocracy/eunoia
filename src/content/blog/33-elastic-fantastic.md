So we were in Queenstown and had to do the extreme sport that makes Queenstown famous all over the world BUNGY!!! . We woke up at around 9 and got ready as usual and headed off to the “Station” where we would catch a bus up to the Nevis cable car. I was looking forward to it as I love the adrenaline rush of jumping off stuff but Murray was not as he gets vertigo and isn’t too fond of jumping off stuff. We got on the bus and headed off to the bungy with encouraging music being played like: ACDC - Highway to Hell, U2 - Vertigo, Bob Marley - Every little thing, Tom Jones - Mama told me not to go and R Kelly – I believe I can fly. We both found this rather funny as did the other people on the bus, we even came up with other good songs they could play like: Feeder – Tumble and Fall etc. To get up to the bungy centre we had to take a steep gravel path which was very rickety and close to the edge. Once up there we were weighed and put in full body harnesses we then went out to an overhanging ledge which looked out over to the Cable car and the valley below. From here we watch several people jump which was cool because you could see just how far they go down but also kind of scary because it’s so high. By this point I was still ok but Murray was wondering why he had agreed to do it. After waiting on the ledge we went across on a small cable car to the large one. On the way over you could look right down which was nice. When we got into the large Cable car we waited for our names to be called out and then had ankle braces put on our legs. Because Murray weighed more he went first. He was made to first sit in a chair and have the bungy cord attached, after a briefing on how to release the cord for his feet he was made to shuffle towards the edge. At this point he did not look to happy and was told to move even closer to the edge. Once there they started a countdown 3,2,1 and then he fell/jumped off and flapped his arms like a chicken (see video) He could not release the cord for his feet so had to be pulled up upside down which meant he came up with a red face which was funny. After a few other people had jumped it was my turn. I went through the same procedure and could feel my hart going as I sat in the chair getting ready. I jumped off well and also managed to release my feet cord so went up sitting (see video). The feeling of falling is indescribable as I have never felt anything like it before and the adrenaline rush is unbelievable. I would do it again any day as it’s just so crazy and I would recommend it to anyone who visits Queenstown!
