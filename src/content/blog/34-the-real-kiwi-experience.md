Whether it was finding ourselves deep underground in freezing black waters or the next day hurtling through white foam on rapids used in shooting Middle Earth; from wandering around stunning rainforest with glaciers hanging from the mountains above; dressing as a shark in a village with a population of one; or simply munching down on green mussels whilst 3000m high peaks rose from the seas around us, one element never faltered: the land of the Kiwi never disappoints.

Since we last posted an article our lives have been packed with awe and adrenaline. The north island always has something bizarre and unique to make your heart pump; the south is simply stunning with everything from dusty plains to lush beech forests carpeted in lichens and mosses.

White water rafting in River Valley was spectacular. I’ve been rafting before but never on a river rated “Grade 5: technical” nor have I been hurled backwards off the edge of a small waterfall and enjoyed it so intensely.

Our brief encounter with Wellington was one, unfortunately, of tiredness and even though we never did find the gourmet burgers the city was friendly and extremely pleasant to wander around.
The ferry itself was a little tedious but well worth the time once we entered the Sounds in the South Island with intense blue waters and towering invasive pine escarpments.

Heading inland into the majestic South island, or Mainland, we came to the wholesome town of Nelson where we stayed a night in a brilliant little hostel with free laundry and internet. You could almost feel sorry for all those we went to the recommended hostel as by this point we had become a two-and-a-half coach group of 100+ backpackers.

Jet boating on the river near Westport was one activity we had never expected. The thrill from whipping downstream, totally drenched from the spray, as the thickly forested valley sides hurtled past was totally exhilarating and unique. Even greater was the rush from suddenly being spun 360’ only to have the full wake slam down over your head! A shame Westport itself wasn’t as exciting…

Lake Mahinapua, total population: Les Lisle. What a character, what a bizarre place. A hostel in the middle of nowhere, whose only business comes from the Great Green Bus, which served one of the greatest BBQ’s known to man and offered a greater amount of alcohol than the entire north of England.

From the Pau Pub to the Possum Café and another hilarious old man led us through the tales of brave Kiwi men that would leap from helicopters onto the backs of wild deer and wrestle them to the ground. Sadly out of his famous possum pies though.

Franz Josef is a place of true beauty, a strange mix of ice and forest high in the Southern Alps. Our full day glacial hike was certainly memorable; the shocking colour of mud-free blue ice is intense.
Leaving FJ we headed out through some of the most stunning scenery in New Zealand. Unfortunately we can’t validate that statement as it was pouring for practically the whole journey but the Pancake Rocks were certainly strange – like the Giants Causeway had been taken, trampled down and split apart.

Wanaka is stunning. Set between huge mountains at the base of one of the largest lakes in the southern hemisphere fed by glacial waters I think it is the place I would have most liked to explore longer…there or perhaps our next stop, Queenstown…

…the true adrenaline capital of the world. Within half an hour of arriving we had already taken a gondola high up into the surrounding mountains to once more dive behind the wheel of a Luge. Was it as good as Rotorua? Not quite. Was it made even harder/more dangerous by the wet tracks? My double somersault in my cart would suggest yes. Was it still incredibly good, cheap fun? 100% of course!

Other activities in Queenstown include jet skiing and jet boating (plus every other kind of water sport), the world famous Canyon Swing, numerous hikes and mountain bike trails, canyoning and waterfalling, hang-gliding, skydiving, skiing/snowboarding (in winter) and of course it’s bizarre child, Bungy. Want to know what possessed us to leap off a cable car and plummet 134m held only by a line of rope? I have no idea, try reading Mike’s blog. But we did it and it was the most immense rush I’ve ever felt…after the initial feeling of “Argh”.

Milford Sound was superb and easily worth the daytrip; the drive alone from Queenstown to the coast was worth the price tag. The native beech forests of New Zealand are simply incredible and the granite slopes of the Southern Alps are completely alien and yet familiar at the same time. We even briefly glimpsed the bizarre alpine parrots, the Kea.

In short New Zealand has been breath taking in both sense of the phrase. We’ve made some pretty good friends and seen some amazing sights. Hopefully the rest of the trip will be just as good or else we’ll question why we ever left the land of the long white cloud.
