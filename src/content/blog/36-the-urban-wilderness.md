The element of Australia that has surprised me more than any other would be the wildlife. Having arrived from New Zealand where, despite its publicity for the quirky and bizarre, the woods and fields are teeming with the same birds I would find in my own back garden, the sheer strangeness and variety of creatures in Oz blows the mind.

Walking around Sydney one feels compelled to be struck by the culture or the people, with its eloquent Opera House and myriad of perfectly lit, well maintained and accessible landmarks around every corner. Yet what really shone for me weren’t the maze-like malls, the views from the Tower or the bustling activity; it was the botanical gardens. Much like Central Park felt in New York, it is almost surreal to step from metropolis to forest and back again in the space of ten minutes. However, unlike Central Park, the gardens in Sydney appear to have grown without restraint, absorbing cathedrals and housing blocks alike and resulting in being very easy to turn a corner and get lost in. To be able to re-enter the city, walk ten minutes directly away and still find yourself at a park entrance is strange indeed; to then walk inside and see several hundred bats, each the size of a small dog, beginning to wake up for the night is even more bizarre.

From cockatoos that would come so close as to peck my camera, ibis that would follow people they knew for minutes, swifts nesting in the side of 17th Century manor homes or even just a plover that refused to move from the middle of a busy path wildlife appeared to be everywhere.

This trend has not let in, either. Reaching Byron Bay and heading into the mountains for a few hours biking was something completely new; hurtling down old fire breaks in plantations or through windy forestry paths cut into the few hectares of remaining pristine forest was different but on par for expectations. Seeing a wild koala lazily watch us go by several metres high into a Eucalyptus or having a kookaburra steal our lunch whilst we played with a leech was not.

Even just within the township itself we see parrots with rainbow chests flying past as though they were starlings; or brilliantly white or pink cockatoos unlike anything I’ve seen before hassling picnickers by the beach. And that’s not even mentioning the occasional dolphin sighted whilst on a walk to the lighthouse before the heavens opened, or swimming with turtles before being barrelled on our way back to shore in a kayak and almost nailing the exit…

Australia has surprised me by being both incredibly wild and immensely Western; it appears to have sacrificed nothing unlike so many other places in the world.
