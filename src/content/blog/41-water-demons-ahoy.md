For quite some time now the overriding element of our time in Australia has been wetness, whether that is avoiding the constant rain and drizzle or splashing around in the sea, water appears to be the main theme. Perhaps then it is fitting that our final excursion in Australia would be one into the Atherton Tablelands, home to Oz’s finest waterfalls, worst waterfalls and just about every other waterfall of note in anyway, ever.

The Tablelands are a coastal mountain range so of course every cloud or slight sea breeze hits them and creates rain; the wettest place in Australia is at its centre and the entire place is covered in fertile farmland or rainforest. They’re also home to some of Oz’s weirdest critters, from frilled lizards to tree kangaroos and of course the one we’d come to see, the duck-billed platypus – obviously, given the wetness.

As ever we set off in the slow morning drizzle of Cairns, travelled through the current “Wettest town of Australia” and up into the Tablelands themselves to visit our first waterfall and swimming hole. Our guide showed us a natural rockslide that was surprisingly good fun and after those swimming had shot down a few times we dried off, ready for our next water adventure. Luckily we had a small group of four plus our driver/guide so we decided to visit a waterfall not normally on the itinerary to kill some time. Unlikely as we arrived it started to rain…and the minibus died an abrupt death. After a while of looking at the falls and trying in vain to get the engine to start up we found a small spot of phone reception and discovered that a faulty safety device was preventing the petrol pump from working because it thought the engine was on fire.

Still we managed to get a lift to the nearby café we were scheduled at for lunch and shortly after the four of us had finished eating our guide rounded the corner in the still limping bus, giving us enough time to visit another waterfall before the replacement from Cairns could catch up. Thus, as the drizzle returned once more, we entered a town whose Aboriginal name means Many Waterfalls for our final scheduled waterfall of the day and one very strange centennial statue (check the photos).

From there we went to our second swimming hole, this time a large crater lake filled with soft-shelled turtles that breath through their bums and archer fish that spit water at passing insects with incredible accuracy. After a while of swimming around with them it was time to begin our return journey back to Cairns, but first the most important stop to see a true water demon: the platypus!

In total we saw four or five male platypus all swimming around the same pool, which is incredible when you know that very few people ever see them in the wild. We never saw too much as they only surface to take a breath before diving back down to forage through the silt at the bottom of the pond. The platypus, quite a fitting animal to sum up our time in Australia: totally bizarre, completely wild and 100% saturated.
