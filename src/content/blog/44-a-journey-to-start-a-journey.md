The journey began one humid, dusky morning in Singapore. It’s 6am and we’ve forgotten our only map; on top of this we can’t remember which subway station we need to be at. In vain we try to communicate our troubles through the language barrier to an official but ultimately just give in, purchase our ride to where we believe we should be going, and slump down to the platform.

One minute. Two minutes. Three minutes and the train arrives, its doors opening with a mechanical swish. There are no seats; it’s six in the morning but somehow our carriage is full. We stand for four stops, our fully laden backpacks becoming increasingly heavy yet finally we alight and leave the Singapore MRT for the final time.

Heading out through the metal turngates we tracked down another innocent transport officer and once more enquired as to where the main train line was. To our eternal gratitude he replied it was close, just take a right and head on down. Finally some good news and we set off, heads high back into the morning humidity.

Yet nothing is ever as easy as it first appears in Singapore and it wasn’t long before doubts began creeping back into our minds. Did he mean right straight out of the station? Or right at the end of the road? If it was so close why were there no signs? Hesitantly Mike asks a fellow pedestrian where the train station was but her only insistent reply comes as “No English, sorry”. Our paces ever slowing we head onwards, clinging to what little hope is left and beginning to thumb through our wallets for taxi fare.

As the end of our road beckons we come upon a young man in army uniform seated in a bus shelter; our last hope. Do you speak English? Yes! Do you know where the train station is? Back the way we came…oh. Wait, we don’t mean the MRT, the Malaysia link train? It’s straight ahead? Take a right down there? Yes we can see the turn, thank you. Thank you!

Once more buoyed by our luck we strike forth and valiantly turn right at the junction. Walking briskly we cover ground fast, but this still looks wrong. Where is this station and why does this look so run down for such an important service? There, look, the logo from the website; where? Ah I see. We cut through a hedge and there in front of us at last is the Malaysia link train. The time? 6:50am: plenty left to work out the bizarre customs forms and to find our carriage amongst the randomly labelled collection of numbers and letters.

This journey had ended; our time in Singapore was up. But as the train pulled away and the carriage attendant finally confirmed we were in the right place we could sit back and muse on the new journey to come. The journey to Malaysia and all its cheap, cheap offerings.
