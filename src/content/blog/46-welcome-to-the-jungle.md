Since we left Jo and Sean in the sunny Caribbean every new place has been, by definition, a new experience for me. So it was with a strange mixture of trepidation and excitement that I landed, once more, at Kota Kinabalu International Airport.

On the one hand it was nice landing somewhere and knowing the only option was a taxi, rather than finding out two days later there was a metro line just round the corner; somewhere I could already source cheap, good food; somewhere I vaguely understood. However it has been three years since I was last in Borneo: how much could have changed?

The answer was surprisingly little; apart from a sudden influx of KFC’s citywide KK is pretty much identical to when I left. So it was with a strange, giddy feeling of nostalgia that we wandered down to the waterfront and ordered a very nice dinner whilst knowing full well the last meal I’d eaten in Borneo was the sweet’n’sour prawn at the very same place. The only difference was this time I couldn’t afford the Tiger beer and had to settle for a coke!

Of course as we awoke to our first Bornean morning and headed out onto the streets everything began slotting back into position. There were the markets I bought Mike’s blo-dart gun in; here’s the City Park Inn where I stayed; there are those same old faces trying to flog fake DVD’s (“DVD! DVD sir! Finest quality, latest releases! DVD?”) on the street corner. The sights and indeed the sounds of Kota Kinabalu were all as remembered; not even the shops in the mall.

However one aspect had changed. This time there were two of us, not fourteen; there were no over-bearing adults around to tell us when and where to go; there was no helping hand and no back-up money; in short there was no Outlook Experience. Which has been refreshing and certainly cheaper, unfortunately it also led us to believe that we could do everything Outlook had done equally well and better. Maybe we should have done more research, or even just some research…

The main problem was that KK became boring. For me there was the nostalgic “oh here’s where Nic got conned into buying seven mangoes” style element, for Mike there was the whole, first-timer “argh I’m trapped in an alley with hundreds of people yelling DVD at me…and Murray’s laughing!” element. For both of us this got old fast. I think last time we did it right, we were only ever in KK for about three days at a time, it kept it fresh and exciting. We did five on the trot and just resorted to watching (and buying) films.

So it was that we decided to go see Sabah; we couldn’t afford to climb the mountain but we could go and take photos of it! Jumping on a rickety little mini-van we shot off up towering mountains, over ridges with incredible views spanning away on each side, eventually arriving at the colossal Mount Kinabalu! And then the bubble burst.

Personally, I blame Hostelworld.com. Up until this point we’d pretty much given up on Google and just used them to source cheap, good accommodation. Unfortunately they don’t cover a small national park in northern Sabah so we had to revert to archaic Google which got us nowhere. Determined not to succumb to analogue channels we just arrived in the Park without a room or knowledge of hostels beyond a few names. To be fair we had found a review online that suggested accommodation could be found for around 12RM and even if it was a few years old how much more expensive could it be? Answer: a lot. Ten fold in fact, and we do not have the capacity to extend our budget that much.

Luckily we did find somewhere to stay, just outside the park, for a decent price. Weirdly it was the one place in Borneo that I had never expected to revisit… an almost home-stay like hostel where we had a wonderfully mad Kiwi to share a room with and a gang of evil looking chickens roosting beneath our floor boards. For anyone who remembers, it was the hostel where the toilet block’s supporting column had snapped leading to some mind-bending angles within… oh and nothing’s been fixed, they’ve just buried the column in a pile of dirt to try and stop it bending anymore! The tailless cat’s also still around, FYI.

So in the end we had a pretty good time in the Park. The accommodation, though much maligned on the previous visit, was just as nice as I personally remember it and just as close to a small café that still serves fantastic fried noodles. Off, then, to Poring Hot Springs!

The hot springs, it turns out, have been bought by the same people that own all the hostels at the Park as well, so once more ludicrous prices. Luckily our bus driver knew a local home-stay where we have our very own, comfortable double bed to share! We’re sitting in it now, typing away at our respective blogs and listening to the night-time rain outside. On the whole we appear to have fluked our way through this part; all we have to do now is get back to KK which, presuming your reading this on the blog and not my recovered hard-drive, we’ve managed to do. The one thing I would change? Bring more money. I have stacks of it in the bank, so why didn’t I bring some more? And why do the two largest tourist destinations in Sabah not have any ATMs!

Sigh, oh well, chalk up another life experience, I guess.
