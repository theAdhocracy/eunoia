The Caribbean was an experience I don’t think I’ll ever quite match again; New Zealand was mind blowing; Australia was very yin-yang with incredibly varying scenery and culture, some that grated, some that hit just the right blend. Singapore was frantic and insanely fun; Kuala Lumpur had chilled back culture and sweet as food; and Borneo, Kota Kinabalu…I guess it was what I remembered. Perfectly; not a single atom has changed. The cheap places are still dirt cheap, the beer’s still insanely over priced, the people are still very friendly and the whole place just works in a strange, haphazard kind of way.

Coming back with Mike has been interesting and I will continue to pester him to create his own blog on this portion of the trip just to get a fresh perspective on things. For me it’s been 100%, completely, a trip down memory lane and because of that it felt a little less like an accomplishment. From revisiting the Mountain and Poring to winding up accidentally choosing the same island in TAR as last time, there’s been a lot of repetition for me.

Not that I’m complaining; if there’s one thing this revisit has reinforced it’s that I really like this country. And we have done things differently, we had to by the very nature of the trip. I didn’t stay in overpriced hostels in Poring, we effectively slept in a homestay with the noise of the kids in the room next door until they were put to sleep and the little café we couldn’t afford to eat at out front. The TAR trip may have been to the same island but it had a completely different feel largely because we were merely day-tripping; plus all the jellyfish forced us to use the beach far more than the sea.
Plus of course there has only been the two of us so wherever we’ve been there have been new people, travelers and locals alike; we can’t book out an entire room or hide within the group, there aren’t enough of us, so in that sense the feel of the country has probably been a lot more rugged and a lot more real as a result.

However I think the over riding factor for me is that KK has been a welcome break; somewhere we arrived and new what to expect (or at least I did), knew where to go and what to do. It’s made a serious change from the constant rushing and jumping around we’ve been doing since leaving New York and that alone has made it completely worthwhile. At the end of the day its nice to have just watched a few DVDs and catch up with friends back home, even if that did mean just spending half a day sitting around the hostel. And on top of that, hopefully, Mike’s been able to see a little bit of the map not normally on the gap-year path. So yeah, I would say this is one return trip that has been a success.
