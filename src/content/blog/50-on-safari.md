We arrived at Cape Town International Airport at around 8pm after a tiring plane journey which saw us change planes at Doha and drop people of in Johannesburg. In total it took something like 18hours and that was just the flight time! Once out of the airport we were picked up by Murray’s Grandparents and taken back to their bungalow in the Noordhoek. For the next few days we recovered from the flight and relaxed.

After Chillaxing for a while we headed off on a road trip, with Murray’s Grandparents in which we travelled to Karoo National Park, Mountain Zebra National Park, Addo Elephant National Park, Plettenberg bay, Oudtshoorn and Prince Albert. During this trip we manage to rack up about 1000 miles which gives you a good idea about how crazy big South Africa is. It was also very different from what I thought it would look like, then again I didn’t really have any idea of what it would look like.

Anyway, on our way before reaching Karoo National Park we went through some really awesome scenery like massive mountains sometimes caped with a sprinkling of snow, autumn coloured vineyards as far as the eye can see and scrub land in the Great Karoo that stretches for miles and miles. The weather was also brilliant and we had blue skies most of the way. Once we arrived at Karoo National Park we booked into our chalets and then went for a drive around the park. The park itself looks kind of like the Grand Canyon but on a smaller scale and more scrub like and it has a few animals too like veret monkeys, kudu, red hartebeest, springbok and ostrich, we saw all of these in this park, some more than others. The next morning we got up early and went round the other end of the park before leaving to drive to the next place which was the Mountain Zebra National Park. Here we stayed in a really scary old ranger’s house which was situated a few minutes into the park. It also had no showers and when we tried to have baths there was not enough hot water and it took an age for them to fill up. However the food at the restaurant was nice and I had some nice kudu stake and Karoo Lamb. We stayed in this park for 2 nights and on the first morning we had veret monkeys come close to the house which was cool. We also saw much more game in this park like zebra, wildebeest, dassies, bush buck, eland and an aardwolf as well as the animals we saw in the first park. This park was nicer to drive around and we even went on a night safari which was freezing cold and we saw most of the animals we had already seen as well as a gemsbok with squint horns.

Once we had spent 2 days at the Zebra Park we headed to Addo Elephant Park which was really cool as we saw 4 of the big 5, lion, buffalo, elephant and rhino. We stayed in nice little round huts which looked straight onto a waterhole which when we arrived had 3 elephants drinking from it. After see those 3 we saw loads more all over the park. We spent 3 nights in Addo and I had a really good time there. Driving round the park was good fun as there were always lots of animals about and the new animals we saw were black back jackals, lions, rhinos from a distance, elephants, meerkats, and many more which I can’t remember the names of. The main reason for Addo being so good was because during a morning safari we saw a pride of lions eating/guarding a dead zebra. It was amazing to see apart from the smell which was sick. The male lion also got a bit angry at one of the male cubs and had a bit small scrap with it which was quite scary as we were only a few feet away and he was growling and stuff. We also came across elephants crossing the roads several times which meant we got to see them up close. The only this that sucked was the amount of Kudu as they were everywhere!

Once we had finished with the game parks we travel to Plettenberg bay where we stayed at a seaside hotel called the singing kettle. We had very nice room which had a TV and a balcony, we enjoyed the TV more as we could watch fake wrestling which is funny. During the next few days we went to the mouth of the storm river and went across a cable suspension bridge there. The sea was also quite ruff so there were lots of waves crashing on the rocks near the river mouth which was cool. We went to a monkey and bird parks which were close to each other which was quite good but I was sick of animals after. We also went to Robberg which is just a point on the coast which is nice to walk round and you can see seals and sometimes white sharks. The biggest bungee jump in South Africa was in the area we were in and we did go and see it but I decided not to do it for 3 reasons. 1, it claimed to be the highest in the world and it’s not. 2, someone has to come down once your left hanging to pull you back up upside down. 3, the freefall was 8 seconds long the same length of the one I did in NZ and I really wanted to fall for longer. But don’t worry mum I’m going to Switzerland at some point to do the golden eye dam bungee and then to china where the highest one in the world is!

We then travelled further along the coast and get to our next and destination which was Oudtshoorn, where we also had very nice accommodation. We went caving which was cool because we got to climb through small holes and watch fat people struggle through them and ostrich riding which was strange and funny. After Oudtshoorn we went up over and down the other side of a mountain to get to our final destination which was Prince Albert where we stayed just one night and then headed back to Cape Town. The road trip was tiring and good fun and accommodation and food where 10 times better than any hostel we stayed at, so thank you Murray’s Grandparents for the trip! Oh and the weather was amazing throughout!

Other than the road trip we have spent most of our time lazying around the house as the weather has not been good but we did manage to spend a day in Cape Town down at the waterfront. We caught the train in and then a taxi to the waterfront where we went to the aquarium and looked round shops and stuff and saw to seals which are a common sight there. Oh and we went to a pancake restaurant which was very nice and a market place. The waterfront was very nice but we were told that muggings were common in Cape Town and on the trains so we had to watch out, but nothing bad happened so it was fine. We also went to Cape Point which was really hot and we got some really good photos, but we didn’t see any sharks or whales.

So anyway a good time had in SA and big thanks again to Murray’s grandparents for driving us around and feeding us.
