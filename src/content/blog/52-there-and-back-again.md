It has been roughly 4008 hours since that fateful morning in February when the entire nation of England was brought to a halt by about one millimetre of snow.

3984 hours have passed since we first stepped onto the well trodden carpets of an air plane bound for great lands and incredibly frustrating customs laws.

After spending many weeks working and laughing under the scorching sun of St.Maarten, it has now been around 2832 hours since we waved goodbye to Jo and Sean and struck out by ourselves in the great beyond.

Approximately 2808 hours ago we lay exhausted after once more battling our way through Americas bizarre and often contradictory laws and found our way to our first hostel two blocks from Central Park.

Roughly 2784 hours ago we felt the pain of our first blisters after woefully underestimating just how far “only one block” could be!

Then, around 2712 hours ago we briefly did not exist on this Earth as we crossed the meridian on our way to New Zealand and lost a day of our lives!

Shortly after, 2582 hours ago, we clambered on board our first Kiwi Experience bus with trepidation and unfounded fear, a step that would lead us to many great friendships.

Over the next 552 hours we would climb glaciers, jump from 132m high cable cars, abseil into the depths of the earth and even stand where Aragorn stood and defied Lord Theoden to ride the Rohirrim to WAR!

Then we left, landing possibly 2040 hours ago Down Under to enjoy the spoils of British conquest in the 18th Century.

Approximates place the next event roughly 12 hours later, when we became confused as to why Oz Experience did not live up to the high standards of its sister company.

1680 hours ago we were sat eating pizza in Newcastle, half way round the world yet seemingly only 1 hour from where we call home…

Around 1248 hours ago I breathed once more under the ocean waves as green sea turtles, black tip reef sharks and thousands of tiny fish swam around the intensely coloured coral of the Great Barrier Reef just inches from my snorkel mask.

Watching a small pond in farmland near the Northern Territories, 1080 hours ago we briefly glimpsed the beak of the worlds second rarest monotreme and winner of the “Weirdest thing in Nature” award 27 years in a row, that patch-work collaboration of genuine, real animals; ladies and gentlemen may I present the Duck-Billed Platypus!

Then we went to South East Asia and so roughly 1032 hours ago ate our first decent meal since Newcastle; though still consisting largely of noodles it readily bested what had become Mike’s staple diet of Maggi and water.

After a further 504 hours had passed we could look back and retell stories of accidentally skipping passport control into Malaysia, having our feet nibbled my small fish for half an hour, those particularly modern cabs we caught to Mt.Kinabalu and back (and the toilets we had whilst up there) and of course the constant awesomeness of every piece of food we could find.

Then as the final leg inched closer we endured 24hours of airports and planes before, around 528 hours ago, we waved to my Grandparents through the plexiglass at Cape Town airport.

384 hours ago we watched a male lion bring the smack down to one of his cubs whilst the pride feasted on dead zebra.

288 hours ago we watched lots of monkeys, then even more birds, then all those same monkeys again, then stroked some cheetahs and wild cats, before finally going home (to Mike’s delight).

Between the times of 264 hours ago and 240 hours ago we rode ostriches and slid through tiny holes deep underground.

Around 72 hours ago we ate pancakes in front of Table Mountain whilst listening to seals play in the waterfront before us.

At some point 24 hours ago we waved a fond farewell to my grandparents.

12 hours ago we touched down on British soil once more.

And now, finally, at this time, we can claim to have not only been There… but also to have come Back Again!
